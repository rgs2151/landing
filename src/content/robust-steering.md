Brains and large language models need not be considered equivalent systems to reveal an interpretable commonality: both exhibit high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics whose behavior can only be modeled imperfectly. This creates a shared challenge whenever we seek to intervene in their dynamics. A controller may either rely on a model identified around a particular operating regime and risk failure as the system moves away from that regime, or explicitly account for uncertainty and a changing internal state. Neurostimulation has confronted an analogous problem for decades. The limitations of fixed, open-loop stimulation have motivated closed-loop and state-dependent approaches that condition intervention on the ongoing state of the neural system. Inspired by this principle, we formulate LLM steering as a feedback-control problem in which the network’s internal state, rather than a fixed intervention alone, determines how the system is perturbed. We develop a robust controller that explicitly accounts for uncertainty in the model’s dynamics, outperforming existing steering methods under distribution shifts. We further introduce robust steerability, a pre-deployment measure of how much uncertainty an intervention can withstand, establishing a foundation for understanding when control remains reliable beyond the conditions in which it was designed.
