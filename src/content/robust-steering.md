A successful perturbation is not the same as reliable control. The effect of stimulation depends on the system's state, so identical interventions can produce different behavioral outcomes. In language models, shifts in prompts, context, or internal activity change how an intervention propagates, altering the dynamics of control itself. Biological circuits and language models are different systems, but both are high-dimensional, nonlinear, and imperfectly modeled, making reliable intervention a shared challenge. We develop a robust controller that adapts to the model's state and accounts for uncertainty, outperforming existing steering approaches under distribution shifts.
