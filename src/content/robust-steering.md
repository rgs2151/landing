A successful perturbation is not the same as reliable control. First, the effect of stimulation depends on the state in which it is delivered: across invasive and non-invasive paradigms, state alters the magnitude, direction and behavioral consequence of otherwise identical stimulation. At deployment, shifts in prompt, context, or activation trajectory alter the network’s local Jacobians and therefore how the same intervention propagates. Distribution shift thus changes not only the state being controlled but also the dynamics of control itself. Language models and biological circuits are not equivalent systems, but both are high-dimensional, nonlinear and imperfectly modeled, and intervention in both inherits the same reliability problem. We develop a robust controller that adapts to the model's state and accounts for uncertainty, outperforming existing steering approaches under distribution shifts.
