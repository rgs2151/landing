Choices and response times are observable outcomes of an otherwise hidden decision process. I built PyLBA to connect these behavioral measurements to a mechanistic account of how evidence accumulates toward competing responses. The package implements the Linear Ballistic Accumulator model in Python, bringing simulation, Bayesian parameter estimation with PyMC, and prediction from fitted parameters into one workflow. Researchers can specify shared or choice-specific parameters, fix known quantities, and define custom priors to investigate evidence accumulation, decision thresholds, and non-decision time. By pairing an explicit generative model with inference and reproducible simulations, PyLBA makes it easier to test how proposed decision mechanisms give rise to observed choices and response-time distributions.
